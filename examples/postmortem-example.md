## Summary

An automated client tried thousands of passwords against /login from a single IP address for about 20 minutes. No account was compromised. The attacking IP was blocked at the firewall.

## Impact

Login latency rose from 120 ms to 900 ms for roughly 15 minutes. No data was accessed and no accounts were taken over.

## Detection

Sentinel's "Brute-force login attempts" rule (5 failed logins from one IP in 60 seconds) raised a HIGH alert 5 minutes after the attack started. The alert was seen on the live dashboard feed.

## Root cause

The login endpoint had no per-account lockout and the global rate limit was set too high (300 requests per 15 minutes per IP) to slow a password-guessing tool.

## Response

The IP was blocked at the firewall, the login rate limit was lowered, and the affected accounts were checked for successful logins from the attacker's address (none found).

## What went well

- The alert fired within minutes and was visible on the live feed.
- The audit log made it easy to confirm that no account was accessed.

## Lessons learned

- A high global rate limit does not protect individual login attempts.
- We had no runbook for blocking an IP quickly.
